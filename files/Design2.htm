Joseph Roche

Joseph is a researcher and lecturer at Trinity College Dublin.

Joseph is a Professor in Education and Associate Dean of Research at Trinity College Dublin.

He leads international research projects investigating the role of science in society and provides workshops on academic skills such as writing, publishing, research funding, communication, and engagement.

Joseph has worked at NASA, was a Visiting Scholar at Harvard, and is an Honorary Research Associate at University College London.

He is the author of the textbook Essential Skills for Early Career Researchers, published by SAGE, which includes more than 30 resources for early career researchers.

Joseph co-leads the Science & Society research group at Trinity College and welcomes queries from prospective doctoral and postdoctoral researchers who are interested in the following topics: citizen science, science communication, higher education science, informal science learning, and public engagement with science.

Research

Joseph leads the Science & Society Research Group at Trinity College Dublin — a multidisciplinary research group that strives for a more inclusive and resilient relationship between science and society. Joseph explores the relationship between science and society through a number of complementary research areas:

  • Citizen Science
  • Science Communication
  • Higher Education Science
  • Informal Science Learning
  • Public Engagement with Science
03
Workshops

Workshops

Joseph carries out public lectures, talks, science shows, and workshops in universities, research centres, and museums around the world.

He utilises audience response systems to facilitate feedback and interaction and has used this approach to compare audience engagement at music, arts, and science festivals (Roche, Stanley, & Davis, 2016) as well as performing science shows with audiences of up to 1,900 people at a time (Roche, 2015; Roche, Cullen, & Ball, 2016).

To discuss potential workshops or to make a booking, please contact joseph@josephroche.ie.
  • Workshop 1: Academic Writing Workshop

    Joseph leads academic writing workshops for researchers in all disciplines. He specialises in helping to simplify the process for those who may not have that much experience in academic writing and has developed an Academic Writing Handbook for people working in museums.

    Joseph’s workshops focus on participants getting hands-on experience writing, reviewing, and editing, and include components on:

    • How to develop academic writing skills
    • Becoming more productive and motivated
    • Structuring papers and other writing tasks
    • Outlining, drafting, reviewing, and editing
    • Establishing writing groups
  • Workshop 2: Grant Writing Workshop

    Joseph has worked as a grant writer for large-scale research proposals for more than a decade. He has worked for the European Commission in a variety of roles related to research funding, including serving as an expert evaluator of research funding proposals and being an external reviewer of pan-European research projects.

    In his grant writing workshops, Joseph focuses on efficient approaches to grant writing — helping researchers to create strategies that allow them to link, build upon, or reuse existing applications to help grant writers who struggle with ceaseless deadlines. Key topics covered include:

    • Identifying funding opportunities
    • Writing to the funding call
    • Brokerage and establishing partnerships
    • Liaising with gatekeepers
    • Creating robust funding plans
  • Workshop 3: Communication & Engagement Workshop

    Joseph’s workshops on communication and engagement draw on his experiences as both a researcher and a practitioner.

    Through his research of participatory forms of communication and engagement, Joseph demonstrates the advantages of empowering audiences. From his years of engaging with the media, Joseph uses real-world examples to illustrate the challenges of successful media engagement as well as how to cope when things go wrong. Workshop topics include:

    • Models of communication
    • How to discuss, debate, and argue
    • Social media
    • Legacy media
    • Public presentations
